## 1. Purpose
This document defines early-stage documentation risk signals identified through simulation and research. These signals are **indicators of potential narrative gaps** that may affect how care records are interpreted during CQC inspections or internal audits.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Risk signals are NOT deterministic rules.** They do not mean care was delivered incorrectly or that a safeguarding breach has occurred. They simply indicate: *"This record may require closer review."*

---

## 2. What Is a Risk Signal?
A detectable pattern in documentation that suggests **missing clarity, incomplete context, or potential inconsistency.**

---

## 3. Signal Categories (Initial Set)

### 3.1 Missing Narrative Detail
**Pattern:** An incident is recorded, but no supporting description is provided.
*   **Example Signal:** `Incident logged` → `No explanation of events`
*   **Interpretation:** The event exists but lacks the "story" required for understanding.
*   **Why It Matters:** Reviewers cannot assess severity, response appropriateness, or follow-up actions.

### 3.2 Internal Inconsistency
**Pattern:** Recorded observations conflict with documented care actions.
*   **Example Signal:** `Observation: "Client in distress"` → `Task: "No intervention recorded"`
*   **Interpretation:** A disconnect exists between what was seen and what was done (or documented as done).
*   **Why It Matters:** Creates ambiguity around responsiveness and quality of care delivery.

### 3.3 Context-Deficient Severity
**Pattern:** An incident is assigned a severity level but lacks supporting justification.
*   **Example Signal:** `Severity: High` → `Narrative: [Blank or Minimal]`
*   **Interpretation:** The classification is present, but the reasoning is invisible.
*   **Why It Matters:** Reviewers cannot validate if the severity was appropriate or if escalation was required.

---

## 4. Important Boundaries
These signals are **heuristic (pattern-based)** and intended for pre-review visibility.
*   ✅ **DO:** Support professional judgment and highlight potential gaps.
*   ❌ **DO NOT:** Automate safeguarding decisions or infer negligence.
*   ❌ **DO NOT:** Replace human accountability or professional expertise.

---

## 5. Role in the CareScribe Simulation
Within the simulation, these signals are used to:
1.  **Highlight** potential documentation gaps in real-time.
2.  **Test** whether care professionals agree with the flagged areas.
3.  **Explore** how structured visibility affects the perceived "completeness" of a record.

---

## 6. Research Direction & Limitations
*   **Current State:** Signals are based on early assumptions and synthetic scenarios.
*   **Validation:** Real-world testing with care professionals is ongoing to identify "false positives."
*   **Goal:** Identifying which signals provide the most practical value to a busy care team.

> **Key Principle:** Signals highlight where documentation may be **unclear** — not where care is **incorrect**.
